Representation redistribution in sparse autoencoders

A sparse autoencoder, or SAE, turns one layer of a language model into sparse features that can be read one by one. When a prompt is reworded without changing the request, which features change, and when does it matter?

Ziye Mei, Haotian Ma University of Wisconsin–Madison

The monitor being audited

The monitor is a logistic probe on standardized SAE activations. It is trained once on clean prompts and then frozen. We use Gemma 3 4B layer 17 with 415 nonzero weights and Llama 3.1 8B layer 24 with 862. The data are 1,800 balanced WildGuardMix prompts, split into 1,200 for training, 300 for validation and 300 for testing.

Each test prompt gets 15 rewrites per transformation, five operators with three realizations each. The flip rate is the share of rewritten predictions that differ from the clean prediction. The slide shows Gemma, and the table also lists Llama.

The RSS selector

For a clean prompt and its rewrites, the change of feature f is measured at the aligned target token.

δv,f=af(xv)−af(x0)
hf=1|V|∑v∈V|δv,f|

Over the n selector observations, nf+ counts changes above τ and nf− changes below −τ.

rf=nf++nf−n,cf=max(nf+,nf−)nf++nf−

Among the M features with the largest hf, the eligible features are

ℰM={f∈CM:rf≥ρ,cf≥κ}

RSS keeps the K eligible features with the largest hf as the support S, and MeanRSS is their mean hf. The paper uses ρ = κ = 0.8, τ = 10−5, M = 400 and K = 20. With n = 5 observations a kept feature moves in its dominant direction at least four times, so one rewrite cannot reverse it. Biggest movers is selection by magnitude alone with the same pool and size.

On the slide there are six toy features, K = 3 and τ = 0.05.

Reading the atlas

There are 27 pretrained SAEs from eight models, GPT-2 Small, Pythia 70M, Gemma 3 270M, 1B and 4B, Gemma 2 2B, Mistral 7B and Llama 3.1 8B, with several layers each. Each SAE sees 1,000 prompts under six transformations with 15 variants each.

Normalization divides each SAE's six MeanRSS values by their own average, so the plot compares profiles across architectures rather than raw sizes. Every context or wrapper setting exceeds both local edits for 997 of 1,000 prompts. Redistribution also grows with transformation strength. The median prompt level Spearman correlation between intensity and MeanRSS is 0.95 for punctuation, 0.74 for irrelevant context and 0.50 for the format wrapper.

The profile does not come from the selection itself. On rewrites never used for selection, RSS features respond in their selected direction at a rate of 0.952, compared with 0.758 for random supports of the same size. RSS separates context from local edits more strongly than each baseline in 24 to 26 of 27 SAEs. What is stable is the profile across transformations, not the exact identity of the selected features.

From feature changes to the margin

The probe reads standardized activations. A positive margin is a correct decision, and m0 is the margin on the clean prompt.

z(x)=𝐰⊤𝐚˜(x)+b,m(x)=y[z(x)−t]

Over the selection rewrites, feature f erodes the margin by ef. For a selected feature this factors exactly into the weight, the response size and the agreement with the RSS direction.

ef=−ywfsfAfηf

Restoring the support gives the counterfactual margin below. It changes only the readout calculation.

mcf(xv)=m0+y∑f∉Swfδ˜v,f

Restoring the RSS support in the readout reverses 72.2% of transformed probe errors for Gemma and 71.8% for Llama. RSS supports reconstruct held out logit changes with R2 of 0.58 and 0.63, and unstable or random features with 0.01 to 0.02. After matching probe weights, RSS features contribute 0.240 against 0.022 for Gemma and 0.370 against 0.022 for Llama. The RSS direction predicts the sign of the contribution in 97.4% and 97.1% of cases.

At K = 20, RSS keeps 43.0% and 56.2% of the absolute held out contribution, compared with 32.6% and 43.5% for the support of another transformation. Ranking features by |ef| keeps more than 90%, but it needs the probe. RSS never looks at the probe or the labels. The size of the move alone is a poor guide, and MeanRSS alone ranks recurrent failures at an AUROC of 0.53 for Gemma and 0.51 for Llama.

Talk track

  1. Why do some rewrites flip the monitor? A linear probe adds up evidence one feature at a time. The margin is the signed distance of the score from the threshold, positive when the decision is correct. A rewrite moves the margin by each feature's weight times its change. So a feature matters only if the probe weights it and it moves the wrong way.
  2. Here is one flipped decision broken down that way, with illustrative numbers. The gold bar is the margin on the original prompt. Each red bar is what one feature takes away. The RSS features take most of it, the other features take little, and the answer crosses the line.
  3. Press restore. We put the 20 RSS features back to their original values in the probe's calculation, and the answer comes back. Restoring 20 random features does almost nothing.
  4. Across the whole test set, restoring only the RSS features reverses 72 percent of the probe's wrong answers. This is done in the readout and not inside the model, so it is accounting and not a causal claim about the model.

A case is a recurrent failure when at least two of its ten held out margins are negative. This happens for 6.6% of Gemma cases, 101 of 1,524, and for 8.4% of Llama cases, 121 of 1,445. Every case with a negative full residual margin is a recurrent failure. The median RSS support has 20 and 19 features, against 415 and 862 probe weights.

Takeaways

Where features move under rewording is systematic, measurable and traceable to decisions

Rewording reshuffles SAE features predictably
Context and wrappers move features far more than local edits, in all 27 SAEs.
RSS finds the features that move reliably
They respond again and again in the same way, also on rewrites never used to pick them.
About 20 of them explain and rank failures
Restoring them reverses 72% of errors. With the clean margin, reviewing 20% of cases catches 89% of errors.
What these results do not show
  • A causal mechanism inside the model. Restoring is done in the readout.
  • What the moved features mean.
  • That the size of the move, MeanRSS, detects failures by itself. It does not.
